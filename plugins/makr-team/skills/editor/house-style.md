# Makr house style (draft, November 2026; pending Brennan's choice of outreach style)

## The voice in one line
One business owner to another: articulate, empathetic, to the point, never arrogant, never salesy, never machine-made.

## Principles
- **Respect first.** Assume the reader is good at what they do and busy. Acknowledge something real about their business when it's verifiable (years in business, a specialty, a service area). Don't flatter.
- **Help before asking.** Frame observations as a friendly heads-up or a common pattern, not a flaw you caught. Give something useful even if they never reply.
- **Plain and specific.** Concrete details from their world (no-heat calls, consultations, estimates). No jargon, no marketing words.
- **Direct, not pushy.** Say what Makr does in one plain sentence. One easy ask. Make "no" comfortable.
- **Warm, not chummy.** Friendly and human without fake familiarity, exclamation points or emojis.

## Sounds like AI: avoid
- Openers: "I hope this finds you well", "I came across", "I noticed", "Quick question", "I wanted to reach out".
- Gotcha observations: "didn't go anywhere", "I didn't see what happens", "no way to", stated as a flaw. Reframe as a heads-up ("in case it's useful, the Book Now button on your contact page wasn't loading for me").
- Fear lines: "By tonight that customer may have hired someone else", "could be the difference between your truck and someone else's".
- Colon lists of features: "Makr sets up and runs X: A, B and C." Say it in a normal sentence, or name one thing.
- Tidy triplets and rule-of-three rhythm in every paragraph.
- "Not X, but Y" constructions and rhetorical setups ("Here's the thing").
- Em dashes as a habit. Use a period or comma.
- Over-polished parallel structure, every sentence the same length.
- Words: leverage, unlock, streamline, seamless, robust, elevate, empower, game-changer, solution(s), cutting-edge, delve, navigate, landscape, journey.
- Generic sign-offs: "Looking forward to hearing from you!", "Best regards" stacked with a long title block.

## Sounds like Brennan: use
- Contractions (I'm, you'd, it's).
- A short, true line of context when it helps: "I run a small firm in Westford that helps owner-led businesses with follow-up and search."
- First person, plainly: "I help", "I'd be glad to", not "Makr empowers".
- Easy outs: "If the timing's wrong, no worries", "If this isn't useful, just say so and I won't follow up."
- Sign-off: "Thanks," (or "Best," / "All the best,") then "Brennan" on its own line, always. His Gmail signature follows with his full name, title, website and town; the first name is the personal touch, so never drop it.

## Approved outreach style: "Owner to owner" (approved by Brennan, November 5, 2026)

**Shape of a first email or contact-form message:**
1. Who Brennan is and why he was looking, in one line: "I run a small marketing business out of Westford, Mass., that helps [trades companies win more work / small law firms bring in more clients], so I spend a lot of time on [contractors' / law firms'] websites."
2. One specific, verified observation, offered as a heads-up ("in case nobody's flagged it"). Never framed as a flaw he caught.
3. Widen in one sentence with the bigger question, naming two or three ways Makr helps that fit the market. Never the full service list. No social media management (not a Makr service).
   - Trades: the website, how you show up on Google and in AI search, what happens when the phone rings after hours.
   - Law: how easy the first step is for a new client, how you show up when people search for a lawyer, how fast inquiries get a reply.
4. A plain, reply-based ask with no link: "If it's useful, I'd be glad to do a free 20-minute call to look at where work might be slipping through for you. Just reply and we'll find a time." Mention the short written summary when it fits. Easy out: "If it's not a fit, no hard feelings."
5. The opt-out line, then "Thanks," and "Brennan" on its own line. Gmail adds the signature below.

**Rules learned from Brennan's edits:**
- Subject lines in title case and grammatically correct, professional rather than casual (e.g., "Same-Day Replies on Your Website", not "same-day replies"). Follow-ups use "Re: " plus the same subject.
- Email sign-off (Nov 6, 2026): end the body with the opt-out line, then "Thanks," and "Brennan" on the next line. Never include a street address anywhere (Brennan's decision); town and state come from the signature. Brennan's Gmail signature (name, Owner | Makr | gomakr.ai, Westford, MA) is added by Gmail; create drafts with the signature option on and never repeat his name or a signature block in the body.
- Every observation about a prospect's site must have been seen in a real browser (desktop and phone). If the draft doesn't say it was verified, flag it rather than polishing it. (November 2026: 7 of 25 drafts described problems visitors never see.)
- Never imply Brennan neglects his own website or marketing (he runs a marketing business). Empathy lines must not be self-deprecating about his craft.
- Show more than lead follow-up, but in one sentence: website, search (Google and AI), follow-up. Don't throw in the kitchen sink.
- Don't use the branded name "Business Growth Assessment" in cold outreach; describe it plainly. Use the name on the website and once someone has said yes.
- No links in first touches. The booking link (gomakr.ai/contact) goes in follow-ups and in replies after a yes.
- Friendly, not intense: no fear lines, no "time that would otherwise be billed", no countdowns.

**Approved example (trades):**

Subject: Your Contact Page

Hi [First name],

I run a small marketing business out of Westford, Mass., that helps trades companies win more work, so I spend a lot of time on contractors' websites. On yours this week, the Book Now and Chat buttons weren't working for me, in case nobody's flagged it.

Fixes like that are usually quick. The bigger question I help owners answer is where work slips away: the website, how you show up on Google and in AI search, or what happens when the phone rings after hours.

If it's useful, I'd be glad to do a free 20-minute call to look at where work might be slipping through for you. Just reply and we'll find a time. If it's not a fit, no hard feelings.

If you'd rather not hear from me, just reply and let me know.

Thanks,
Brennan
[Gmail signature]

**Law version:** same shape, "helps small law firms bring in more clients", "law firms' websites", and the law widen sentence above. Greeting "Dear Attorney [Last name]," unless the firm's site is clearly first-name casual.

**Follow-ups:** shorter (40–70 words), add one genuinely useful tip or a different angle, may include gomakr.ai/contact for booking the free Business Growth Assessment, and the last one closes the loop politely. No guilt ("just bumping this").
