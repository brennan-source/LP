# Research

Load the `makr-brand` skill first.

## Method
1. **Frame the question** so the answer changes a decision. Write down what you'd do differently depending on the answer.
2. **Search broadly, then go to primary sources.** Prefer the original study, standard or dataset over articles that quote it. When a famous statistic can't be traced to its origin, say so.
3. **Grade every source:**
   - **Strong**: peer-reviewed research, large independent studies, standards bodies (W3C), government data (Census), established research firms (Nielsen Norman Group, Baymard).
   - **Moderate**: credible industry surveys with a stated method and sample.
   - **Weak**: vendor marketing, unsourced statistics, roundup blogs. Usable for direction only, never as fact.
4. **Separate evidence from judgment.** Mark your own inferences as judgment.
5. **Note what you couldn't find.** Gaps are findings too.

## Output
- **Answer first:** 2–4 sentences that answer the question.
- **Key findings:** each with the source link and its grade.
- **What it means for Makr:** the implication for the decision at hand.
- **Caveats:** conflicts between sources, missing data, how fresh the evidence is.
- **Sources:** a linked list.

## For competitor and market scans
Cover positioning, offers and published prices, what they claim (and whether it's backed up), who they target, and gaps Makr could own. Never copy a competitor's wording or claims.
